Research

We connect single-cell technology development with cancer biology and liquid biopsy, from identifying rare malignant cells to understanding their molecular and functional diversity.

Research

Research directions

We study how malignant cells can be identified in accessible specimens, how their molecular and cellular states vary, and how these differences relate to diagnosis and treatment.

DIRECTION 01

Rare tumor cells in body-fluid specimens

Rare malignant cells can be difficult to distinguish from benign cells using morphology or conventional markers alone. We develop cell-based screening approaches and use single-cell DNA sequencing to examine genomic copy-number alterations as evidence of malignant identity.

Our studies include circulating tumor cells in blood and exfoliated tumor cells in bronchoalveolar lavage, urine, pleural effusion, and ascites. These different specimen contexts require appropriate sampling, cell selection, and molecular validation.

DIRECTION 02

Sequencing-guided cytopathology AI

Reliable image annotation is essential for diagnostic AI, particularly when tumor cells are rare or resemble benign cells. We connect single-cell sequencing results to the corresponding cell images, using molecular evidence to guide cancer-cell annotation.

Our bronchoalveolar lavage work uses this approach to develop deep-learning models for lung cancer cytopathology. We evaluate cell classification and specimen-level diagnosis separately, with clinical cohorts used to assess diagnostic performance.

DIRECTION 03

Tumor heterogeneity, plasticity, and microenvironment

We combine single-cell and spatial analyses to investigate how genetically related tumor cells adopt different states and interact with their surroundings. Research questions include clonal relationships, lineage plasticity, and the spatial organization of tumor and stromal cells.

Our work on combined small-cell lung cancer examines the relationship between mixed histology, a shared clonal origin, neuroendocrine plasticity, and distinct microenvironmental niches.

DIRECTION 04

Single-cell function and treatment response

Molecular identity alone does not capture the functional diversity of tumor cells. We study glucose uptake, mitochondrial activity, and functional protein expression at single-cell resolution to characterize metabolic and signaling states.

Studies of lung cancer cells in pleural effusion investigate how these phenotypes relate to treatment response and clinical outcomes. This work links functional heterogeneity to clinically relevant questions and the investigation of response-associated biomarkers.

Methods

Tools for studying individual tumor cells

Our methodological foundation includes microchip-based single-cell assays, multiplexed protein measurements, metabolic phenotyping, and rare-cell retrieval for genomic analysis. These approaches make it possible to connect functional measurements with molecular information from individual cells, while sequencing and image analysis support cancer-cell identification and cytopathology research.

The lab’s broader research interests include tissue-of-origin identification, treatment-response prediction, and neoantigen vaccine design, within a wider program of precision diagnostic systems for major diseases.
