Terminology

CTC usually refers to an intact tumor cell circulating in blood or lymph. In bronchoalveolar lavage fluid, urine, pleural effusion, and ascites, this portal uses the broader and more precise term “body-fluid tumor cells.”

Specimen sources

Five fluid contexts

Collections in preparation
BLD

Peripheral blood

Circulating tumor cells and associated blood-cell populations sampled through liquid biopsy.

BALF

Bronchoalveolar lavage

Airway and alveolar cells collected from bronchoalveolar lavage fluid for lung-focused analysis.

URN

Urine

Urine-derived cells offering a non-invasive view of urinary tract tumors and their environment.

PLF

Pleural effusion

Tumor and microenvironment cells from pleural fluid, often associated with thoracic malignancy.

ASC

Ascites

Malignant and immune cell populations from peritoneal fluid in abdominal and pelvic cancers.

Scientific context

Look beyond blood,
closer to the tumor.

Non-blood fluids can provide cellular information from anatomically relevant environments. Their composition and collection conditions differ substantially, so the atlas keeps specimen-specific metadata visible rather than merging all cells under one generic CTC label.
